Robot learning on a desktop arm

Teaching a low-cost robot arm new skills from demonstrations and simulation, then measuring how well those skills hold up on the real hardware.

Role
Personal project
When
September 2026 to present
Hardware
SO-101 arm (LeRobot kit); desktop with an RTX 5070 Ti for training
Software
LeRobot, PyTorch, NVIDIA Isaac Sim and Isaac Lab
Status
Teleoperation working. Recording demonstrations next.

Why this project

Most of my robotics work so far has been the software around the robot: perception, control loops, and the pipelines that move data. The controllers were hand-tuned. Robot learning replaces some of that tuning with policies learned from data, and I want to understand it by building the whole loop myself, on hardware I can afford to crash.

The plan

  1. Teleoperate. Done Drive the arm by hand through LeRobot. Working now, as in the video above.
  2. Record demonstrations. Next Capture repeated attempts at one task, such as picking up a block, as training episodes of camera frames and joint positions.
  3. Train an imitation policy. Planned Fit a policy such as ACT or Diffusion Policy to those episodes so the arm can repeat the task on its own.
  4. Train in simulation. Planned Train a reach policy in Isaac Lab with randomized physics and visuals, so it does not overfit to one simulated world.
  5. Transfer and measure. Planned Run each policy on the real arm and log its success rate over repeated trials.

How I'll judge it

Success rate over repeated trials on the same task and setup, for every policy. One clean video can hide a policy that fails one time in three. The numbers go on this page as they come in, failures included.
