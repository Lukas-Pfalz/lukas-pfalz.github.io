Autonomous rover with a voice

A ground rover for safety operations that finds a target with its camera, drives to it on its own, and holds a spoken conversation, with everything running on the robot.

Role
Data science intern, KBR
Program
Multi-Domain Autonomous Command and Control
When
June to August 2026
Hardware
Jetson Orin NX, Astra Pro Plus RGB-D camera, ReSpeaker mic array, Ackermann-steered chassis
Software
ROS 2 Humble, YOLO26 on TensorRT FP16, Whisper, a local language model, Piper

Employer project. Details are kept at a general level.

Detect, track, drive

The rover's first job is to get to the right place. The camera finds the target, a tracker keeps the rover on that one target, and a control loop turns the target's position in the frame into steering commands, 20 times a second.

  1. See. The Astra Pro Plus streams color and depth images into ROS 2.
  2. Detect. YOLO26, exported to ONNX and TensorRT FP16, finds targets in real time on the Jetson.
  3. Track. A single-target tracker holds onto one target, so the rover does not jump between detections.
  4. Steer. A 20 Hz PID loop steers to keep the target centered, with the IMU gyro providing heading feedback.
  5. Drive. Commands go to the Ackermann steering and drive motors.

Hardest part

Small corrections disappeared. The steering servo ignores very small commands, so the fine adjustments that keep a target centered never reached the wheels. I measured that deadband and reworked the steering command path so small corrections get through.

Talking with the rover

Once it arrives, the rover has to find out what a person needs. The voice agent asks triage questions, listens, and keeps a transcript that becomes a short report for a human responder. Speech recognition, the language model, and speech synthesis all run on the Jetson, with no cloud connection.

  1. Listen. The ReSpeaker mic array captures speech and estimates which direction it came from.
  2. Detect speech. Voice activity detection decides when someone is talking.
  3. Transcribe. Whisper turns speech into text.
  4. Respond. A language model running on the Jetson, prompted as a triage assistant, writes the reply.
  5. Speak. Piper turns the reply into speech through the rover's speaker.
  6. Report. The conversation is saved and summarized for a responder.

Hardest part

Fitting it all in 8 GB. Speech recognition, the language model, speech synthesis, and the detector share the Jetson's 8 GB of memory, and a model that loaded on an idle board failed on a busy one. I quantized the model's cache, shortened its context, and reordered startup so the largest model loads first.

Result

Camera frame to steering command at 20 Hz on the Jetson, and spoken conversations handled on the robot itself.
